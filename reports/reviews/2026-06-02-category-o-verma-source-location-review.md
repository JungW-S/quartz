## Source Locations

### 1. Category $\mathcal O^q$

Hong-Kang 2002, Section 3.2, printed p.43:

- Defines, for $\lambda\in P$,
  $$
  D(\lambda)=\{\mu\in P\mid \mu\le \lambda\}.
  $$
- Defines the category $\mathcal O^q$ as consisting of weight $U_q(\mathfrak g)$-modules $V^q$ with finite-dimensional weight spaces and weights contained in a finite union
  $$
  \operatorname{wt}(V^q)\subset D(\lambda_1)\cup\cdots\cup D(\lambda_s).
  $$
- The same page first defines weight modules, weight vectors, maximal vectors, weight multiplicities, and characters.
- Proposition 3.2.1 on p.43 states that every submodule of a weight module over $U_q(\mathfrak g)$ is again a weight module.

Safe future page scope:

- Define $\mathcal O^q$ exactly in Hong-Kang's quantum-group notation.
- Explain that this is the quantum-group version of the category used in the book.
- Do not present a broad BGG Category O survey unless a separate source is approved.

### 2. Highest-weight modules inside $\mathcal O^q$

Hong-Kang 2002, Section 3.2, printed pp.43-44:

- After defining $\mathcal O^q$, the source says that the most important examples among $U_q(\mathfrak g)$-modules in $\mathcal O^q$ may be highest weight modules.
- It defines a highest weight module of highest weight $\lambda\in P$ by the existence of a nonzero vector $v_\lambda\in V^q$ satisfying
  $$
  e_i v_\lambda=0\quad(i\in I),
  $$
  $$
  q^h v_\lambda=q^{\lambda(h)}v_\lambda\quad(h\in P^\vee),
  $$
  and
  $$
  V^q=U_q(\mathfrak g)v_\lambda.
  $$
- The highest weight vector is unique up to scalar multiple.
- The source records:
  $$
  \dim V^q_\lambda=1,\qquad
  \dim V^q_\mu<\infty,\qquad
  V^q=\bigoplus_{\mu\le \lambda}V^q_\mu.
  $$

Safe future page scope:

- `Category O` may refer back to highest-weight modules as the basic class of examples.
- `Highest-Weight Modules` already contains a compact version of this material, so the Category O page should avoid duplicating the full definition.

### 3. Verma module construction

Hong-Kang 2002, Section 3.2, printed p.44:

- Fixes $\lambda\in P$.
- Defines $J^q(\lambda)$ as the left ideal of $U_q(\mathfrak g)$ generated by:
  $$
  e_i\quad(i\in I),
  $$
  and
  $$
  q^h-q^{\lambda(h)}1\quad(h\in P^\vee).
  $$
- Defines the Verma module:
  $$
  M^q(\lambda)=U_q(\mathfrak g)/J^q(\lambda).
  $$
- Sets
  $$
  v_\lambda=1+J^q(\lambda).
  $$
- Verifies:
  $$
  q^h v_\lambda=q^{\lambda(h)}v_\lambda,\qquad
  e_i v_\lambda=0,\qquad
  U_q(\mathfrak g)v_\lambda=M^q(\lambda).
  $$
- Concludes that $M^q(\lambda)$ is a highest weight module with highest weight $\lambda$ and highest weight vector $v_\lambda$.

Safe future page scope:

- `Verma Modules` can be filled definition-ready from this source.
- Use Hong-Kang's quantum notation $M^q(\lambda)$, $J^q(\lambda)$, and $v_\lambda$.
- Do not import classical Verma module conventions unless a separate source is approved.

### 4. Universal property and maximal submodule

Hong-Kang 2002, Proposition 3.2.2, printed pp.44-45:

- As a $U_q^-$-module, $M^q(\lambda)$ is free of rank $1$, generated by $v_\lambda=1+J^q(\lambda)$.
- Every highest-weight $U_q(\mathfrak g)$-module with highest weight $\lambda$ is a homomorphic image of $M^q(\lambda)$.
- $M^q(\lambda)$ has a unique maximal submodule.

Hong-Kang 2002, printed p.45:

- Denotes the unique maximal submodule by
  $$
  N^q(\lambda).
  $$
- Defines the irreducible highest-weight quotient
  $$
  V^q(\lambda)=M^q(\lambda)/N^q(\lambda).
  $$

Safe future page scope:

- Include these as theorem/proposition-level facts on the `Verma Modules` page.
- Keep the proof out of the first reader-facing version unless explicitly requested.

### 5. Integrable subcategory

Hong-Kang 2002, Definition 3.2.3, printed p.45:

- Defines the main category studied in the chapter:
  $$
  \mathcal O^q_{\mathrm{int}}.
  $$
- A $U_q(\mathfrak g)$-module belongs to $\mathcal O^q_{\mathrm{int}}$ if it satisfies the $\mathcal O^q$ weight-space conditions and all $e_i,f_i$ are locally nilpotent.
- Morphisms are usual $U_q(\mathfrak g)$-module homomorphisms.
- The source records that $\mathcal O^q_{\mathrm{int}}$ consists of integrable $U_q(\mathfrak g)$-modules in $\mathcal O^q$, and is closed under finite direct sums and tensor products.

Hong-Kang 2002, Proposition 3.2.4, printed p.46:

- If $V^q\in\mathcal O^q_{\mathrm{int}}$, then for each $i\in I$, $V^q$ decomposes into a direct sum of finite-dimensional irreducible $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$-type submodules for the subalgebra generated by $e_i,f_i,K_i^{\pm1}$.

Safe future page scope:

- The `Category O` page can include a short subsection distinguishing $\mathcal O^q$ from $\mathcal O^q_{\mathrm{int}}$.
- Do not overfill the page with the full integrable-representation theory. That belongs in a later page if it becomes necessary.
